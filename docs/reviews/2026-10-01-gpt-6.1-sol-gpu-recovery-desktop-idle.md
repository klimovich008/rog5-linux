## Round 1

1. **P1 — A later submit can complete a deferred submit without executing it.** [msm_gpu.c:878](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:878), [msm_gpu.c:901](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:901). Submit A fails init and remains queued. Before recovery runs—or between failed recovery attempts—submit B can retry init successfully and submit directly. On the same ring, B’s higher fence makes A appear completed through [msm_fence.c:88](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_fence.c:88). Recovery can then retire A without replaying it. Pending deferred submissions must prevent direct submission until recovery has replayed them in order.

2. **P1 — Successful recovery loses the hangcheck timer.** [msm_gpu.c:905](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:905), [msm_gpu.c:724](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:724), [msm_gpu.c:624](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:624). Failed submission deletes the timer; the new timer callback also returns without rearming it. Recovery rearms only when init fails again. Successful replay therefore has no hangcheck until another submission arrives. A replayed job that stalls without a hardware interrupt can hang indefinitely: the scheduler timeout is [MAX_SCHEDULE_TIMEOUT](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_ringbuffer.c:72), while watchdog health becomes `0 N`. Rearm whenever replay leaves outstanding work.

3. **P1 — Normal reboot stops the watchdog before its SysRq fallback.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:81](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:81), [rog5-gpu-watchdog.service:5](configs/systemd/rog5-gpu-watchdog.service:5). The service retains systemd’s default shutdown dependencies, so an accepted `systemctl reboot` transaction terminates it and its sleeping children. If shutdown subsequently stalls on a GPU client, the scheduled `s/u/b` never executes. The offline test substitutes `touch` for reboot and cannot expose this. The fallback needs an owner that survives shutdown. [Systemd documents these default dependencies.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/man/systemd.service.xml)

4. **P1 — Two unhealthy snapshots can reboot a GPU that recovered between them.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:93](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:93). For example, recovery A briefly overlaps the 60-second sample, completes, and recovery B briefly overlaps the 70-second sample. Both return `0 Y`, causing reboot despite healthy operation between them. Neither the boolean nor the resettable failure counter identifies a continuous incident. Track recovery identity/duration or observe intervening recovery before concluding “dead for at least 70 s.”

5. **P2 — Work starting near the idle deadline can still be killed.** [rog5-desktop-mode:370](scripts/device/rog5-desktop-mode:370), [rog5-desktop-mode:427](scripts/device/rog5-desktop-mode:427). A check at 540 seconds caches “no work”; shader compilation starting at 599 seconds remains undetected until the next poll. If GNOME publishes its idle lock at 600 seconds, the lock branch stops GNOME before checking work. I reproduced this decision with the real functions and mocked services. Preserve the mandatory lock hand-back, but establish the inhibitor before locking can occur; the current polling scheme leaves this window.

6. **P2 — Permanent kmsg read failure silently disables monitoring.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:65](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:65), [rog5-gpu-watchdog:129](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:129). Both reader errors are discarded. An unreadable/missing kmsg device makes `cat` retry forever while keeping the pipe open. With initially healthy parameters, the main process blocks indefinitely and never performs another health check; systemd sees a running service. Distinguish overruns from permanent failure and propagate reader failure.

**CONFIRMED at source level:** both ignored init results and the proposed `gpu->lock`/fault-completion cycle exist. The incident timeline supports that diagnosis, but without blocked-task stacks it does not prove that exact deadlock occurred. The added recovery PM reference is balanced, and deferred submissions retain their active-submit reference and bookkeeping. On A660, `active_ring()` reads a pointer; the new timer early branch neither sleeps nor loops.

**CONFIRMED for inhibitor ownership:** `setpriv`/`env` execute the inhibitor under the tracked PID; normal stop kills and reaps it, and service termination cleans its cgroup. The GNOME self-inhibitor parser excludes its own ID. The two-second completion waits break the described cycle, but remain a timeout mitigation: sufficiently slow legitimate captures can also exceed them.

## Round 2

Round 2 still has two P1 issues. Reviewed the current worktree; no files modified.

1. **P1 — Failed recovery can still let a later submit bypass unreplayed work.** [msm_gpu.c:622](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:622), [msm_gpu.c:900](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:900). A normal hang enters recovery with `deferred_submits=false`. If recovery’s init fails, remaining submissions stay queued, but that flag remains false. A new submission can then initialise successfully and submit directly; its higher fence retires the unreplayed submissions. Set the deferral barrier whenever failed recovery leaves pending work.

2. **P1 — The reset fallback still has shutdown gaps.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:109](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:109).
   - The transient unit survives ordinary service stopping, but lacks `SurviveFinalKillSignal=yes`. Systemd’s final shutdown phase kills it before unmounting; a subsequent shutdown stall loses the reset fallback. [Systemd documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/man/systemd.unit.xml), [shutdown implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/src/shutdown/shutdown.c).
   - If `systemd-run` fails, `setsid` changes the session but leaves the child in the watchdog’s cgroup. The default `KillMode=control-group` kills it when the watchdog stops. [Systemd kill semantics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/man/systemd.kill.xml).
   - `--no-block` confirms that startup was queued, not that the fallback started successfully; execution failures therefore bypass the inline fallback. [Systemd-run documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/man/systemd-run.xml).

3. **P2 — Deferred replay executes work from closed contexts.** [msm_gpu.c:647](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_gpu.c:647), [msm_ringbuffer.c:40](k111+0152 source: drivers/gpu/drm/msm/msm_ringbuffer.c:40). `msm_job_run()` temporarily zeros `nr_cmds` for closed contexts, then restores it. With deferral, no empty submission reaches hardware, and recovery later executes the restored commands because it checks only VM usability. It also misses contexts closed while awaiting recovery. Replay must preserve the existing rule that unwritten work from closed contexts becomes a no-op while still completing its fence.

4. **P2 — Permanent kmsg errors can still silently disable monitoring.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:87](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:87). `-r` does not establish that opening or reading the device succeeds. If `cat` repeatedly fails while `-r` remains true—for example, an `EPERM` from the device’s permission checks—the loop keeps the pipe open indefinitely. A mocked permanent read failure reproduced this: the reader timed out instead of exiting. Distinguish overruns from permanent errors using the actual read failure.

5. **P3 — `reset_script` inserts configurable values as unquoted shell source.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:99](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:99). Defaults render correctly, but `SYSRQ=/tmp/sys rq` generates `>>/tmp/sys rq`, targeting the wrong file. Quotes or shell metacharacters also change the command. Pass values as positional arguments to the child shell and quote their expansions.

6. **P3 — Unexpected `GRACE` values abort Bash arithmetic.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:141](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:141), [rog5-gpu-watchdog:165](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:165). `GRACE=08` reproduces a fatal octal-parsing error; nonnumeric values can trigger nounset errors. Validate and normalise configurable durations. The kernel’s actual decimal `uint` counters fit the device’s Bash integer range.

## Round 3

Four defects remain:

- **P1 — Inline fallback still dies during shutdown.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:129](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:129): when `systemd-run` fails, `setsid` leaves the child in the watchdog’s cgroup. Service stopping kills it under default `KillMode=control-group`. [Systemd kill semantics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/v262/man/systemd.kill.xml).
- **P2 — Blocking start still misses exec failures.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:126](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:126): default `Type=simple` reports success after fork, before exec, potentially bypassing the inline fallback. Add `Type=exec`. [Systemd-run documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/v262/man/systemd-run.xml).
- **P3 — Positional paths still undergo systemd expansion.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:128](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:128): a literal `${HOME}` in `SYSRQ` gets expanded by the manager. Add `--expand-environment=no`. [Systemd-run documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/v262/man/systemd-run.xml).
- **P3 — Duration validation permits integer overflow.** [rog5-gpu-watchdog:38](scripts/device/rog5-gpu-watchdog:38): `9223372036854775808` becomes negative; `18446744073709551616` becomes zero. Bound values before arithmetic.

Confirmed: failed-recovery deferral, closed-context replay, bounded immediate kmsg failures, and ordinary decimal/quoted-path fixes. All five patched files match the patch hunks. Syntax and in-memory probes passed; no files modified or live shutdown tested.
